# Fictional thesis abstract: reading on paper and on screens

## Abstract
This thesis asks whether students read long texts more slowly on a screen than on paper. We compared both formats with 36 second-year students, who each read two articles of the same length.

## Method
We chose a within-subject design because it controls for differences in reading skill between students. Each student read one article on paper and one on a laptop, in random order, and answered ten comprehension questions after each.

## Results
Reading on paper took 14 minutes on average, and reading on a screen took 15 minutes. Comprehension scores were 7.4 out of 10 on paper and 7.1 on screen.

## Discussion
The difference in time was small, and the group was too small to rule out chance. Therefore we conclude that, for texts of this length, the format matters less than many students believe.
